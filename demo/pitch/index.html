These are real messages from one user in the challenge test set. In May she names the cat Fig. In July she calls her Sombra. In September she asks for the name. Two hundred conversations later, an assistant has to pick one, and it has to show where it got it.

Long chats break memory.

  1. Facts“What's my cat called?”
  2. Advice“That book you recommended?”
  3. Updates“I moved in July.”
  4. Dates“How long since…?”
  5. Inventions“Remember Rome?”

The five question types of the Reactive AI challenge.

Long chats break memory in five ways, and they're exactly the challenge's five question types: plain facts, the assistant's own advice, updates where the latest value wins, date arithmetic, and the dangerous one, inventing a memory that never happened.

One question, a few pages, a cited answer.

This is the product. A question about two dates. Librarian picks two conversations out of two hundred, our code computes the gap, Reactor answers fourteen and cites both conversations. The dots are the archive: Reactor read 0.2% of it. Under the answer, a memory audit lists every other mention.

How it works

  1. 1Routethe question
  2. 2Search200 chats, locally
  3. 3Pack~3 dated passages
  4. 4Reactoranswers or says “not in memory”

Dates and counts are computed in code.

Under the hood: we route the question to one of five types, search every turn with keyword and meaning search plus a reranker, all on our own models, pack about three dated passages, and Reactor answers with citations or says not in memory. Dates and counts are computed in code, not guessed by the model.

Results. Our offline score went from 42.9 for plain keyword search to 87.1. All 146 test questions were answered by Reactor, 13 of them as not in memory, reading 0.2% of each archive. The trick was scoring evidence packs offline in minutes, so one slow shared Reactor got five rounds of fixes overnight.

Memory with receipts. Try it now at librarian67.pages.dev/demo, the code is open on GitHub. Thank you. Likely questions: 1. Why not just a long context window? 1.6M characters per user don't fit, cost grows with every message, and models lose facts in the middle. We read 0.2% and cite it. 2. How do you know when to abstain? Reactor answers only from the pack; when the fact isn't there it says not in memory. 13 of 146 test questions; 5 of 5 correct on our dev sample. 3. Latency? Retrieval is about a second on a laptop and a few seconds on our hosted CPU; Reactor itself takes 1–2 minutes per answer. 4. Does it work beyond this dataset? The pipeline is generic: any dated chat log. Import of ChatGPT and Claude exports is next. 5. Business model? Per active memory for apps that keep long relationships: companions, coaching, support, personal agents.
